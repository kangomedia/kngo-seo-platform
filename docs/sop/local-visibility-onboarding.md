# SOP — Local Visibility Onboarding Meeting

**Purpose:** A repeatable, industry-agnostic playbook for the first sales/discovery meeting with a prospect interested in KangoMedia's **Local Visibility** plan ($400/mo). The goal of the meeting isn't to sell — it's to qualify, reframe, and earn the right to deliver a baseline. The sale is the natural outcome of doing those three things well.

**Audience:** Freddy (or any future KangoMedia team member running onboarding).

**Outcome of a successful meeting:** the prospect signs the engagement letter and pays month 1 within 48 hours, with realistic expectations about timeline and scope.

---

## Pre-meeting checklist

Do these the morning of the meeting. **15 minutes total.**

- [ ] Look up the prospect's domain. Skim the homepage. Note 2 things they do well and 1 thing that's obviously broken (or out of date).
- [ ] Check their current Google Business Profile — does it exist? Photos? Reviews? Posts in the last 90 days?
- [ ] Pull a quick competitor scan: top 3 results for their primary service + city. Look at the reviews count and the recency of their GBP posts.
- [ ] **Do NOT run a Site Audit or Baseline Report on their site yet.** Those are paid deliverables. Showing them work product before the contract is signed sets the wrong precedent and you risk surfacing data that makes you look bad (especially on a site you just launched — fresh sites have no GSC history, no rankings, and low scores by definition).
- [ ] Open a fresh Note app or this SOP on your second monitor. You'll capture answers in real time.

## Meeting structure (45–60 minutes)

| Block | Time | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Warm-up + agenda | 5 min | Set the meeting up so they're talking, not listening |
| 2. Discovery (the bulk of the meeting) | 25–30 min | Get the answers you need to deliver well |
| 3. Reframe how they should think about local SEO | 5–10 min | Change their mental model so the plan makes sense |
| 4. Walk through what's in the plan | 5 min | What they're paying for, plainly |
| 5. Set expectations on timeline + scope | 5 min | Avoid awkward conversations 60 days from now |
| 6. Close + next steps | 5 min | Concrete commitment + when they hear from you next |

**The 80/20 of running this well:** spend more time listening than talking. If you're talking more than half the time before block 3, you're rushing.

---

## Block 1 — Warm-up + agenda (5 min)

**Don't open with a pitch.** Open with a question.

> "Before I jump into anything, I want to make sure I understand your business well enough that anything I recommend actually fits. Mind if I ask you a bunch of questions for the first 20–30 minutes? Then I'll walk through what we'd do and what to expect."

This does three things: it sets the meeting up as collaborative (not a sales pitch), it earns the right to ask hard questions, and it lowers their guard. People love being asked about their business.

---

## Block 2 — Discovery (25–30 min)

These questions are **industry-agnostic**. They work for an HVAC contractor, a personal injury lawyer, a Pilates studio, or a wedding photographer. Adapt the wording to their vocabulary as you go.

Don't read these like a checklist. Pick the questions that fit the moment. If they answer two at once, skip ahead. If something interesting comes up, follow the thread.

### 2A. The business (5 min)

Goal: understand what they sell, to whom, and what makes them different.

1. **Walk me through your business in 2 minutes. What do you sell, who's your customer, and how do they typically find you today?**
   *Listen for: services, geographic area, current acquisition channels (referrals? Google? ads? events?). This anchors everything else.*

2. **What part of your business do you wish you got more of, and what do you wish you got less of?**
   *This is the goldmine question. They'll tell you their highest-margin service AND the time-wasting calls they're tired of getting. The content strategy should bias toward the first and filter out the second.*

3. **If a prospect picks your competitor over you, why did they pick the competitor?**
   *Forces honest competitive self-awareness. Reveals their actual market position.*

### 2B. The ideal customer (5 min)

Goal: get specific enough that the content team can write FOR a person, not a demographic.

4. **Tell me about your best customer from the last 12 months. Not your favorite — your most profitable. What did they buy, what kind of business or household are they, and how did they find you?**

5. **What's a question you get asked over and over by prospects that, honestly, makes you a little tired of answering it?**
   *These exact questions become GBP Q&As and blog post titles. They have crazy-high commercial intent because real prospects are asking them. Capture verbatim wording.*

6. **What do your happiest customers say about you when they leave a review or a referral? What word or phrase comes up the most?**
   *That phrase is their organic brand. It should show up in their content.*

### 2C. The market (5 min)

Goal: scope geography and competition realistically.

7. **What's the service area you actually want to work in? Be honest — not where you'll drive if you have to, but where you make money?**
   *Critical for local SEO. "We serve all of Colorado" is almost never true and dilutes the strategy. Get the 3-5 zip codes or 2-3 city names where they really want to be #1.*

8. **Who are 2 or 3 competitors you respect — businesses you'd be proud to be confused with?**
   *Better question than "who do you hate." Tells you their aspirational positioning. Look these up after the meeting.*

9. **When you've lost deals you wanted to win, what was usually the deciding factor — price, response time, trust, location, something else?**

### 2D. Growth + goals (5 min)

Goal: get a number. SEO without a target is unmeasurable.

10. **How many new customers a month would feel like real growth — not "transformational," just "this is working"?**

11. **What's the average revenue from a new customer in their first 12 months with you?**
    *You need this to set the ROI bar. If their average customer is worth $5K and we're charging $400/mo, even one new customer in 6 months is 2.5× ROI. If their average is $200, the math is harder and you need to set tighter expectations.*

12. **A year from now, what would have to be true for you to look back at this and say "that was the best money I spent"?**
    *Forces them to articulate the success metric. Write down their exact words. You'll repeat them back in monthly reports.*

### 2E. Content + voice (3–5 min)

Goal: get permission to write in their voice.

13. **Are there topics you absolutely don't want us writing about — competitors, certain services, anything political or sensitive?**

14. **Is there a person at your business who would review the drafts and approve them? Or is that you?**
    *Sets up the content review portal. Get an email address.*

15. **Do you have an existing brand voice — playful, formal, technical — or do you trust us to set the tone based on the customer reviews you described earlier?**

## Block 3 — Reframe how they should think about local SEO (5–10 min)

**This is where you change their mental model.** Most prospects walk in thinking "SEO = pay you to make my site rank #1 for a keyword." That's not what they're buying, and if you don't reframe it now you'll be fighting that expectation for the next 12 months.

Use 2–3 of these reframes, picking the ones that match what they already said:

### Reframe 1 — "Local SEO is a compounding asset, not a paid ad"

> "When you run a Google Ad, you pay for clicks. The moment you stop paying, the clicks stop. Local SEO is the opposite. Every piece of content we write, every review we help you earn, every directory listing — those stay live, indexed, and earning compound interest for years. The first 60 days won't feel like much. By month 6, the same work we did in month 1 is still pulling traffic. That's the trade."

### Reframe 2 — "Your Google Business Profile is your real homepage"

> "For local businesses, your Google Business Profile is where 70%+ of new customers will first meet you — not your website. The website is where they verify you're real. So the most leveraged thing we do every month isn't a blog post — it's making sure your GBP has fresh posts, fresh photos, a stream of new reviews, and Q&As that answer the actual questions buyers are asking before they call. That's what gets you in the Map Pack."

### Reframe 3 — "Content is how you stop competing on price"

> "Right now, if a prospect searches '[your service] near me' and you pop up next to 5 other businesses, the only thing differentiating you is reviews and price. Once we publish content that answers their real questions — the ones you mentioned earlier — they show up to the consultation already convinced you know what you're doing. That changes the conversation from 'how much' to 'when can you start.'"

### Reframe 4 — "Press releases don't bring traffic. They build trust and authority."

> "Press releases used to be about getting featured in the local paper. Today, they do something different. When we publish a press release, it gets distributed across a network of news sites and picked up by Google. That gives you backlinks from authoritative domains — which Google reads as 'this business is legit, other publications mention them.' That signal moves the rest of your content up the rankings. So you won't get a flood of customers from one press release. You'll get one slow, steady tailwind that lifts everything else we're doing."

### Reframe 5 — "Reviews are an SEO ranking factor, not just social proof"

> "Most people think of reviews as something prospects read before buying. They are — but they're also one of the strongest local ranking signals Google uses. Volume of reviews, recency of reviews, and whether you respond to them all factor in. Part of what we do every month is help you build a system to consistently get new ones and respond to existing ones."

## Block 4 — Walk through what's in the plan (5 min)

Don't list the deliverables in agency-speak. Walk through what they mean in plain language. Use the table below as your structure:

| What you get each month | What that actually means for your business |
|---|---|
| **2 blog posts** (1,200–2,000 words, SEO-optimized) | Two long-form articles targeting the questions your real prospects are searching. We write them, you review them, then we publish to your site. |
| **2 Google Business Profile posts** | Short updates on your GBP — offers, news, seasonal hooks. Keeps your profile active, which Google rewards with better Map Pack rankings. |
| **2 Google Business Profile Q&As** | We answer the questions buyers ask before they call you, so they don't have to call to ask. Often turns lookers into bookers. |
| **1 press release** | A monthly newsworthy announcement, distributed across a wire network. Earns backlinks from news sites — Google's version of trust signals. |
| **25 directory listings** | We get your business listed accurately across 25 directories (Yelp, Apple Maps, Bing Places, industry-specific ones). Each one is a small SEO signal AND another place a customer might find you. |
| **Monthly performance report** | One report each month showing what we did, what's ranking better, and what's driving traffic. |

**Tell them the work is unified, even though the tools aren't.** They have one contract, one invoice, one report, and one point of contact (you). Don't volunteer the partner-tool plumbing unless they ask — and if they do, frame it as "we use best-in-class tools for each piece, because nobody is the best at everything." Be honest if pressed; be efficient if not.

## Block 5 — Set expectations on timeline + scope (5 min)

This is the most important block. Bad expectations kill more agency relationships than bad work.

### Timeline reality, said out loud

> "Here's the honest timeline. The first 30 days are setup — we'll baseline where you are today, optimize your Google Business Profile, get the first wave of directory listings live, and ship your first round of content. You probably won't see traffic move yet. **The first 30 days are about putting machinery in place.**

> "Months 2 and 3, you'll start to see the GBP gain traction. More views, more directions, more calls. The blog posts will get indexed and start ranking for low-competition terms. **This is when you'll feel like something is happening.**

> "Months 4 through 6 is when the content compounds. We've now got 8–12 pieces of content earning traffic, the GBP is humming, and the press releases have built backlinks. **This is when the math starts working in your favor.**

> "If you're expecting to be #1 for your main keyword in 30 days, we're not the right fit. If you can give us six months of consistent execution, this is one of the best investments a local business can make."

### What's NOT included (be explicit)

Tell them this. Don't let them assume.

- Google Ads / paid traffic management
- Website redesign (the platform tracks content, not site rebuilds)
- Email marketing campaigns
- Social media management (Facebook, Instagram, TikTok)
- E-commerce SEO (different beast — needs Growth tier or higher)
- Local TV/radio/print
- Negative review removal (we'll help you respond; we can't make them disappear)
- Anything that asks Google to break their guidelines (link buying, fake reviews, doorway pages)

### What we expect from them

Equally important.

- **One person** who reviews content drafts and approves them within 5 business days. Slow approvals are the #1 reason agencies underperform.
- **Honest answers** if something we publish doesn't feel right. Better feedback in month 2 than a quiet cancellation in month 5.
- **Access** to their Google Business Profile (we'll request manager access — they retain ownership), Google Analytics, and Search Console.
- **Patience for the first 60 days** when results are mostly invisible.

## Block 6 — Close + next steps (5 min)

Don't go full ABC ("always be closing"). The work has already been done by being honest in blocks 1–5. The close is just confirming what they already decided.

> "Based on what you've told me, the Local Visibility plan fits — you've got a defined service area, a clear customer profile, and growth goals that justify a 6-month commitment. The next step on my side is: I'll send you the engagement letter and the first invoice today. Once both are signed, here's what happens in the first 7 days:

> 1. **Day 1:** You get a personalized 'Where You're Starting From' baseline report. This is your floor — every monthly report after will measure against it.
> 2. **Day 2–3:** We'll send a short access request for your Google Business Profile, Google Analytics, and Search Console.
> 3. **Day 5:** You get the proposed content plan for month 1 — two blog topics, two GBP posts, two Q&As, and the press release angle — for your approval inside the KangoMedia portal.
> 4. **Day 7–10:** First content drafts hit your portal for review.
> 5. **Day 14:** Directory listings begin going out.
> 6. **Day 30:** First monthly performance report.

> "Any questions about that flow?"

Then ask for the email address they want the engagement letter sent to and confirm a follow-up time within 48 hours.

## After the meeting: the first-deliverable sequence

This is what the client signed up for — make sure it happens **in this order**:

1. **Same day:** Send the engagement letter + first invoice.
2. **Within 24 hours of payment:** Generate the **Baseline Report** in the platform and publish it. Frame it as "Day 1 — Here's where we're starting from." This is when the platform's baseline becomes safe to show, because the contract reframes any "this looks bad" data as "this is the floor we'll improve from." (Per the platform: agency reports list → publish toggle on the baseline.)
3. **Within 48 hours of payment:** Run the keyword research (KNGO portal) to seed the content strategy. Push at least one round of "service" mode + one round of "pain-point" mode keywords through.
4. **Within 5 days:** Generate the 6-month content strategy in the platform. Promote the first month's content (2 blogs + 2 GBP + 2 Q&As + 1 press release) into the May Content Plan.
5. **Within 7 days:** Send the Plan for client review via the public portal link. The client approves or requests edits.
6. **Once plan approved:** Click "Generate All Drafts" on the platform. Walk away. Come back in 5 minutes. Review drafts, then send to client for review.
7. **Throughout the month:** Track directory listings, GBP optimization, and press release distribution in the partner tools. Update the Deliverables tab on the platform so it's all in one report at month-end.
8. **End of month:** Generate the **Monthly Report** in the platform. Publish it. Send the public link.

## Why we don't show the baseline at the onboarding meeting

You raised this concern. Here's the position to take internally:

- A fresh site (or a recently relaunched one) has **no Search Console history, no rankings, and an audit score that reflects "we just shipped it"** — not "we're bad at this." But that nuance is invisible to a non-technical buyer.
- Showing baseline data at the sales meeting puts you in the defensive position of explaining why the numbers are low. Even if you're right, you're now negotiating from a weaker emotional posture.
- Showing baseline data **on Day 1 after they paid** flips the frame. The contract is signed. The data is now the floor — not a critique. Every monthly report after shows growth from that floor, which is exactly what they're paying you to deliver.

So: keep the platform's Site Audit and Baseline Report tabs as **post-payment deliverables**, not pre-payment proof points. Use the competitor scan from your pre-meeting prep (Block 0) as your "here's what your market looks like" evidence instead.

## Common objections + how to handle them

### "Can you guarantee I'll rank #1?"
> "No, and anyone who guarantees that is either lying or planning to violate Google's guidelines in a way that'll get you penalized inside a year. What I can guarantee is consistent execution against a strategy we both agree on. Rankings are the byproduct."

### "Why can't I just do this myself?"
> "You absolutely can. Most of our clients started by trying. The reason they hired us was that the content writing, the GBP discipline, the directory work, and the press release distribution all took 20+ hours a month — and the months they got busy with their own business were the months SEO went dark and rankings slipped. The value we add is consistency. Twelve months of unbroken execution beats six months of great work and six months of nothing."

### "Another agency promised me #1 rankings in 30 days for less money"
> "Then go with them, and call me in 90 days. If they delivered, you saved money. If they didn't — or worse, they did something that got your domain penalized — I'll still be here. I'm not in the business of underbidding people who make promises they can't keep."

### "I just want rankings, not content — can you do that?"
> "Not honestly. Google's ranking system in 2026 is essentially 'who has the most useful content on this topic, backed by trust signals.' There's no shortcut around the content. What we can do is pick the topics where you're most likely to win and write them well."

### "Isn't this just the same as buying Google Ads?"
> "Different financial model. Ads are rent — you stop paying, the traffic stops. SEO is ownership — the work compounds and keeps earning after we stop. Most growing businesses run both, because ads are a faucet you turn on when you need volume now, and SEO is the asset that funds the business when you don't want to keep paying the faucet."

### "Can we start month-to-month?"
> "I'd love to say yes, but the math on this tier doesn't work that way. The first 60 days are setup costs we eat — baseline, audits, GBP optimization, directory setup. We need a minimum 6-month commitment so we both come out ahead. If 6 months feels long, that's a sign Local Visibility might not be the right plan and we should talk about a one-time engagement instead."

### "How does this work with my existing website?" (especially relevant for clients whose site you built)
> "Perfect setup — we built your site, so we already know the tech stack, the design system, and where to publish content cleanly. Most of our SEO clients have to onboard us to a site they don't control. With you, we're already inside the building."

## Red flags — clients we should decline

Not every prospect should sign. Walk away if you hear:

- **They want guaranteed rankings or guaranteed leads.** Future fight you'll lose.
- **They expect results in 30 days.** Expectations mismatch will cancel the contract before the strategy compounds.
- **They've been through 3+ agencies in the last 2 years.** Either they're unreasonable or there's something operationally broken on their end (slow approvals, no GBP access, etc.). Both are bad for retention.
- **Their service area is "all of [state]" or "anywhere in the US."** Local SEO needs focus. Push them to Growth tier or decline.
- **They want to write the content themselves and have you "just publish it."** Different product. Either upcharge or decline.
- **They demand to know exactly which keywords you'll target before signing.** They're either shopping the strategy around or going to micromanage. Both end badly.
- **Their gut says "no."** Trust it. Bad clients cost more than they pay.
